Privacy policy

Perilous Penny · Last updated: 27 November 2026

This explains, in plain words, what Perilous Penny keeps about you, where it keeps it, and the choices you have. The short version: your progress stays on your phone. The game is free because it shows adverts, and the ad network collects some information to show them. Signing in to Google Play Games is optional, and so is personalised advertising.

Perilous Penny is made by Mr Luke Nunn ("we", "us"). Contact: thedevden.io@gmail.com

What the game keeps on your phone

Everything below is saved in one file inside the game's own private storage on your device. Other apps can't read it, and uninstalling the game deletes it.

  • Your progress: levels played, stars, scores and best times, coins, lives, the manor, your pets, trophies, badges, the daily puzzle streak and your season pass tiers.
  • Your hero and profile: the name and nickname you type, optional pronouns and motto, and how your hero looks. You choose all of these, and they can be anything.
  • Settings: sound, accessibility options, performance mode and your privacy choices.
  • Play statistics: totals such as play time, levels won and pins pulled, shown on your profile and used for badges.
  • Difficulty log: for each level, how many tries, wins and losses there were, what caught Penny, how long it took, and whether a hint or skip was used. This helps us find levels that are too hard. It stays on your phone unless you agree to share it (see below).
  • A random player number: made on first launch, used to make your friend code. It isn't linked to your name, email, phone number or advertising ID.
  • Purchase records: which items you bought, and Google Play's reference number for each purchase, so they stay yours and are never given twice. Payment details are never seen or kept by the game.
  • Error log: if the game hits a bug or closes unexpectedly, a short note is saved with the error, the game version and the screen you were on. On the next launch you can save or copy a report to send to us yourself. The game never sends it on its own.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, the game can remind you when your lives are full again, when your daily gift is ready, when a Partner Event starts or is on its last day, and once if you haven't played for a few days. These reminders are worked out and set on your phone when you leave the game. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else to make them. You can turn each kind off in Settings > Notifications, or turn them all off in your phone's settings.

Sharing play data (your choice)

The first time you play, the game asks whether you'd like to help balance the levels. If you say yes, the difficulty log above is sent to us, under a random number that isn't your player number, to improve the levels. It never includes your name, contacts, location or anything you've typed.

It's sent to the game's own database on Google Firebase (see "Friends and Partner Event teams" below), about every six hours while you play, under the game's anonymous sign-in rather than your player number. Along with the difficulty log it includes which screens you open, how many times, and which days you played (counted from your first day, not dates), so we can see where players get stuck or stop. Players under 13 never send anything. Nothing is sent from players who said no.

You can change your answer any time in Settings > Privacy.

Adverts

The game shows adverts from Google AdMob to keep it free: a short ad between some attempts, and ads you choose to watch for a reward (a hint, coins, a life, or skipping a level).

  • What AdMob collects: to show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, your IP address and rough location (from it), information about your device, and how you interact with the ads. Google is responsible for this under its own policy: How Google uses information from apps that use its services. We don't receive this information.
  • Personalised ads are your choice. On first launch the game asks whether ads may be personalised. If you say no, you still see ads, but every request asks Google for non-personalised ads only. You can change this any time in Settings > Privacy.
  • In the UK, the EU and the EEA, Google's own consent message also appears, as the law there requires. You can accept, refuse or close it; refusing or closing means non-personalised ads. You can change that choice from Settings > Privacy too.
  • Your advertising ID can be reset or deleted at any time in your phone's settings (Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Google > Ads, depending on the phone).
  • Remove Ads: buying it stops the ads between attempts for good. Ads you choose to watch for a reward stay on offer.

Purchases

Everything for sale is bought through Google Play. Google handles the payment under its own terms and privacy policy, and the game never sees your card or bank details. The game receives only a confirmation of what was bought and a purchase reference number, which it keeps on your phone so the item stays yours and comes back if you reinstall.

Google Play Games (optional)

If you sign in to Google Play Games:

  • Cloud save: a copy of your progress (everything listed under "What the game keeps on your phone", except the error log) is kept in your Google account's Saved Games, so you can carry on on another phone.
  • Leaderboards: your total stars, best scores and manor repairs are posted to Play Games leaderboards, with your Play Games name and picture. Other players can see them, as Play Games' own settings allow.
  • Google is responsible for your Play Games account and its data under the Google privacy policy. You can see, change or delete your Play Games profile and this game's saved data in the Play Games app's settings.

You don't have to sign in: everything else in the game works without it.

Friends and Partner Event teams

These use our own online database, run on Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore and Firebase Authentication) under Google's privacy and security terms for Firebase.

  • When it starts: only once you open Friends or Partner Events. Until then nothing is sent.
  • Your account: the game makes an anonymous online account for your phone. It has no name, email or phone number. Its login is kept in your save file.
  • What's shared: your friend code, nickname, title, hero look, stars and levels reached, your manor and other homes, and your pets, so friends can visit. During a Partner Event, also your team, invites, event points and furthest room. Anyone who knows your friend code can look at this.
  • What isn't: your coins, purchases, settings, difficulty log or anything else.
  • Deleting it: contact us with your friend code (shown on the Friends screen) and we'll delete your online profile and event entries.

Other online features

  • Game settings updates: the game may download small settings files from us, such as prices or event dates, so we can adjust things without an update. Downloading them sends nothing about you or your progress. Like any download, our server sees your phone's internet address, which we don't keep.
  • Test builds of the game have a pretend sign-in and a pretend cloud that are really just another file on your phone, and show Google's test ads.

Children

Perilous Penny is a puzzle game for everyone, including children. When the game first starts, it asks what year you were born. It keeps only whether you're under 13, never the year itself, and nothing about your age is sent anywhere.

If you're under 13:

  • No play data is shared. The "Help balance the levels" choice is off and can't be turned on.
  • Ads are never personalised. Every ad request tells Google AdMob the player is a child (child-directed, and under the age of consent), asks for non-personalised ads only, and asks only for ads suitable for all ages (rated G). AdMob is certified by Google for children's apps under its Families policy. Google's consent message isn't shown.
  • Online features are off: Google Play Games and our online database aren't started, so there's no sign-in, cloud save, friends, leaderboards or Partner Event teams.
  • Purchases go through Google Play, where a parent can set up approval or limits with Family Link.

The game never asks anyone, of any age, for an email address, phone number, real name or location. (The "name" in your profile can be anything, and it stays on your phone.) We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you think a child's information has reached us, contact us and we'll delete it.

Keeping, changing and deleting your data

  • Reset progress in Settings clears your progress on this device.
  • Uninstalling the game, or clearing its data in your phone's settings, deletes everything it saved.
  • If you shared play data and want it deleted, contact us with your request and we'll delete it. Since it's kept under a random number, telling us roughly when you played helps.
  • Your cloud save and leaderboard entries can be deleted in the Play Games app's settings (delete your Play Games data for Perilous Penny), or ask us and we'll tell you how.
  • Your friends profile and Partner Event entries can be deleted by contacting us with your friend code.
  • Ad data is held by Google, not us: reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings, and see Google's policy above for more.

Under UK and EU data protection law (the UK GDPR and GDPR), you have the right to see, correct and delete your personal data, and to complain to your data protection authority (in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office, ico.org.uk).

Changes

If we change this policy in a way that matters, the game will ask you again before anything new happens.
